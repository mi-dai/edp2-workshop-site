Enabling Time-Domain Science with LSST EDP2

September 22–25, 2026

University of Pittsburgh · Hybrid

Overview

Rubin Observatory's Early Data Preview 2 (EDP2) gives the time-domain community its first encounter with real processed LSST data. The realistic characteristics and volume of EDP2 present challenges not fully captured by simulations and render many existing analysis pipelines impractical. This hands-on workshop unites time-domain astronomers and cosmologists across DESC, TVS, and the SLSC to tackle the analysis challenges posed by EDP2 and a ramped-up alert stream — from transient discovery through spectroscopic follow-up selection — using LINCC Frameworks tools. The workshop will produce reusable tutorials addressing these challenges, seed new cross-collaboration efforts, and build lasting momentum for pipeline development at the Rubin scale.

Goals

The workshop targets common time-domain challenges posed by EDP2 and the LSST alert stream: filtering the dataset to identify transient candidates; classification and light-curve fitting; cross-matching with galaxy catalogs for host-galaxy association and photometry; selecting candidates for spectroscopic follow-up; evaluating forced photometry and scene-modeling photometry requirements; incorporating alert and prompt data products into discovery workflows; and generating realistic simulations to characterize selection effects and correct for biases.

The program combines a small number of invited and contributed talks with dedicated hack days for tutorial development and co-working time. Concrete targets: at least five public tutorial modules and at least three cross-collaboration hack projects that continue beyond the workshop, supported afterward by a dedicated Slack channel, a one-month follow-up call, and a public GitHub repository.

LINCC Frameworks Tools

LSDB / HATS

Large-scale catalog and time-series analysis — filtering EDP2 and alert-stream data, cross-matching against galaxy and redshift catalogs, and light-curve fitting at scale.

LightCurveLynx

Realistic simulations based on EDP2 visit tables, compared against real transient catalogs and other simulation tools (e.g. SNANA).

RAIL

Photometric redshift framework used to obtain photo-z estimates for candidate host galaxies.

Hyrax

Machine-learning inference framework, adapted here to classify EDP2 light curves.

The workshop will also make use of FASTDB (DESC's transient database), Blast for host-galaxy association, alert brokers integrated with FASTDB, the Rubin Science Platform (RSP), and NERSC for larger-scale computation.
